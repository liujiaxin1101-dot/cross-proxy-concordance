## Figure 05 — K-Score vs. D-Score Scatter by Landing Task

| File | `05_paper_figure.png` / `05_paper_figure.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `05_visualize.py` |
| Panel | Side-by-side CMJ (left) and DJ (right) |

**What it shows**: Each point is one subject (N = 22), with K-Score (kinematic composite) on the x-axis and D-Score (kinetic composite) on the y-axis. Both scores are z-score standardized. Points are color-coded by quadrant:

- **Green (Agree)**: Both K-Score and D-Score fall on the same side of their respective medians — the two proxies concur.
- **Orange (K+D−)**: K-Score is high (≥ median) but D-Score is low (< median) — kinematics flag risk but kinetics do not.
- **Blue (K−D+)**: K-Score is low but D-Score is high — kinetics flag risk but kinematics do not.

Dashed grey lines mark zero (z-score origin). Dotted grey lines mark the median-split boundary for each axis. Subject IDs are annotated next to each point. Spearman ρ and percent agreement are reported in the title of each subplot.

**Key result**: CMJ and DJ show moderate rank-order agreement, with 2–4 conflicting classifications per task. The CTL group has 4 conflicts in the full analysis (18.2%).

## Figure 06 — Kappa Forest Plot

| File | `06_kappa_forest.png` / `06_kappa_forest.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `06_kappa_forest.py` |

**What it shows**: Cohen's κ point estimates and 95% confidence intervals across 11 methodological conditions, ordered by effect magnitude. Color codes distinguish:

- **Blue**: Primary specification (IC = 20 N, equal weights).
- **Teal**: Task-stratified analyses (CMJ only, DJ only).
- **Light green**: IC threshold variants (IC = 50 N, kinematic IC).
- **Orange**: Weighting variants (knee valgus × 2, loading rate × 2, K-Score = knee valgus only, D-Score = peak GRF only).
- **Red**: Robustness checks (excluding sub24, direction convention reversed).

Vertical reference lines mark Landis & Koch (1977) benchmark thresholds: Poor, Slight, Fair, Moderate, Substantial, Almost perfect.

**Key result**: κ ranges from 0.091 (kinematic IC, direction incorrect) to 0.636 (primary specification). Only the primary specification and closely related variants cross the "Substantial" (κ ≥ 0.61) threshold. Classification agreement is fragile to analytic choices.

## Figure 07 — Bland-Altman Plot

| File | `07_bland_altman.png` / `07_bland_altman.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `plot_bland_altman.py` |

**What it shows**: Bland-Altman plot of K-Score vs. D-Score differences against their mean, computed on pre-second-z subject-level means (N = 22) to avoid the trivial bias = 0 that arises after z-score standardization.

Solid red line: mean difference (bias). Dashed blue lines: 95% limits of agreement (bias ± 1.96 × SD). Outlier subjects (difference > 1.5 SD from bias) are annotated in red.

**Key result**: The 95% LoA spans approximately [−2.00, +2.00] z-score units, indicating that individual-level disagreement can cover nearly the full range of the sample distribution. Bias is effectively zero, confirming no systematic over/under-estimation of risk by one proxy relative to the other.

## Figure 08 — Quadrant Drift

| File | `08_quadrant_drift.png` / `08_quadrant_drift.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `quadrant_drift.py` |

**What it shows**: Tracks how each subject's (K, D) classification changes under 8 sensitivity conditions (S1–S8). Each subject is a trajectory showing movement across the four quadrants (Agree, K+D−, K−D+, and a central region) as analytic choices vary.

**Key result**: Individual subjects do not stay fixed in a single quadrant; many subjects drift between Agree and Conflict categories depending on the analytic specification. This visualizes the "fragile consistency" core argument — cross-proxy agreement is not a stable property of individuals but varies with methodological decisions.

## Figure 09a — Combinatorial Perturbation Panel

| File | `09_combinatorial_panel.png` / `09_combinatorial_panel.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `combinatorial_visual.py` |

**What it shows**: Two-panel figure showing κ (top) and ρ (bottom) across 9 analytic specifications: 4 single-factor perturbations (Primary, direction reversed, DJ only, K-Score = knee valgus only) and 4 combinatorial perturbations (C1–C4: pairwise and three-way combinations). Point estimates with 95% confidence intervals.

**Key result**: Combinatorial perturbations (e.g., reversing direction AND restricting to DJ) show additive degradation of agreement. κ drops from 0.636 to 0.091 when three perturbations are combined (C4), demonstrating that methodological choices compound to produce fundamentally different conclusions about cross-proxy concordance.

## Figure 09b — Specification Curve

| File | `09_spec_curve.png` / `09_spec_curve.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `spec_curve_plot.py` (data from `spec_curve.py`) |
| Method | Simonsohn et al. (2020) specification curve analysis |

**What it shows**: A specification curve — all possible analytic specifications (combinations of data inclusion, variable selection, weighting, and direction conventions) ordered by their resulting κ or ρ value. The top panel shows the point estimate for each specification; the bottom panel shows which analytic choices are active in each specification. A null distribution (permutation-based) provides an inferential baseline.

**Key result**: The distribution of κ across specifications spans from approximately −0.32 to +0.96. The observed κ = 0.636 from the primary specification is near the upper tail, but the full range reveals that many defensible analytic choices would yield κ values below 0.20. This multiverse-style visualization makes the fragility of the agreement estimate transparent.

## Figure 10 — Pooled Scatter

| File | `10_pooled_scatter.png` / `10_pooled_scatter.pdf` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `pooled_scatter.py` |

**What it shows**: Same as Figure 05 but using pooled CMJ+DJ data (all 6 trials per subject combined before computing scores), yielding a single scatter point per subject. This is the primary specification used for the main ρ and κ statistics reported in the paper (ρ = 0.558, κ = 0.636).

**Key result**: After pooling across landing tasks, 4 out of 22 subjects (18.2%) fall into conflict quadrants — symmetrically split between K+D− (n=2) and K−D+ (n=2). McNemar test confirms no systematic directional bias (p = 0.62).

## Figure — sub24 Diagnostic

| File | `sub24_diagnostic.png` |
|------|------|
| Generated by | `sub24_diag.py` |

**What it shows**: Time-series diagnostics for subject sub24 (extreme outlier, D-Score = 3.54) compared to a representative subject (sub07). Shows vGRF and kinematic angle trajectories across the landing phase for all 3 CMJ trials.

**Key result**: sub24 exhibits substantially higher intra-trial variability (K_raw_std = 1.20, D_raw_std = 1.90, the largest among all subjects) and a D-Score ~3.5 SD above the group mean. Despite this, excluding sub24 has negligible impact on the primary ρ and κ estimates (Δρ = +0.060, Δκ = −0.018), confirming the results are not driven by a single outlier.
